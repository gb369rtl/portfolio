## The problem

A design review can leave hundreds of marked-up comments on a set of drawings. When the next revision arrives, someone has to go through every comment and check that it was acted on. It is slow, and comments get missed.

## Who uses it

Reviewers and document controllers on engineering projects.

## A job with it

They upload the original set, the marked-up set and the new revision. Progress shows page by page. At the end there is a list of comments, each with a verdict and the revised page beside it, and a completion rate for the whole set.

## In scope

Finding markups, matching pages across revisions, judging each comment, and a person's final accept or reject.

## Left out on purpose

Deciding whether the engineering itself is right. The pipeline checks that a comment was acted on, not that the answer is good.

## How success is judged

The share of comments found, the share of verdicts a reviewer accepts as they are, and the time saved on each revision.

## Main risk

Saying a comment was addressed when it was not. Each verdict carries a confidence, cannot verify is an allowed answer, and a person makes the final call.
