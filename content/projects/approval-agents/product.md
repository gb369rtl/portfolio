## The problem

Approvals arrive as free-form email. Someone had to read each one, find the matching record and update it by hand, which is slow and easy to get wrong on a large project.

## Who uses it

Procurement coordinators who own the approval log, and the project leads who depend on it being current.

## A day with it

A vendor replies with a revised drawing. A few minutes later the coordinator sees a card on the review desk: which approval this is for, what the agent proposes, how confident it is, and the evidence it used. The coordinator approves it, corrects the match, asks for a re-run, or discards it with a reason.

## In scope

Reading mail and attachments, matching them to records, proposing the update, human review, and an audit trail of who approved what.

## Left out on purpose

Replying to vendors by itself, and writing to a record without a person signing off.

## How success is judged

The share of messages matched to the right record, the share of proposals accepted without edits, and the time from email to updated record.

## Main risk

A wrong match is the expensive failure. That is why every write is reviewed, every run can be replayed, and the tools the model holds cannot change anything.
