## The problem

A payment form has dozens of fields, and many of them depend on each other. People who raise payments all day still make mistakes, and each mistake costs an approver a rejection and a second round.

## Who uses it

Staff who raise payment requests, and the approvers who receive them.

## A turn with it

The user writes: pay the supplier two lakh by tomorrow from the operations account. The agent fills what it can, asks which supplier branch, checks the amount against the rules, and reads the whole instruction back. The user confirms on screen and submits.

## In scope

Collecting and validating a form by conversation, in text or by voice, and handing over a form that is ready to submit.

## Left out on purpose

Submitting by voice alone, deciding approvals, and anything the form's own rules would not allow.

## How success is judged

The share of conversations that end in a correct form, the time per turn, and the number of wrong values that reach a staged form, which has to stay at zero.

## Main risk

A wrong amount or a wrong account. Three things stand in the way: the rules, the read-back, and the fact that only a person presses Submit.
